Compound risk is the structural condition where a single state is positioned high on multiple, semantically-distinct crisis recipes at the same time: civil-war shape and economic-recession shape and mass-protest shape, layered on the same body politic. Each recipe scores the country independently against its own canonical pre-event trajectory; the compound view stacks those positions and surfaces where the stack is tallest.

The page answers one question: where do structural pressures pile up on the same state?It does not claim those pressures interact causally, nor that the recipes will fire jointly. It is a multi-frame similarity read, surfaced honestly with each recipe's own tier and band carried through.

How to read this board
  1. The compound scoreis a relative stacking of high rankings across this country's lit recipes, not a probability.
  2. A country lit on three recipes is not"more likely to experience all three". The score aggregates independent discrimination ranks, not multiplied likelihoods.
  3. Only foreign_intervention has a candidate deployment today (currently re-validating after a coverage fix). The rest are either tested without holding up, or not yet tested. With one near-deployed recipe, this view is illustrative.
  4. The view becomes meaningful as more recipes are validated and deployed across quarterly reviews.

MOCK NARRATIVE

Analyst-style structural reads on two countries where multiple recipes co-light. These are mock narratives for design preview only, not published QGI assessments. They will be replaced by curated, dated briefs once the recipe fleet deploys a second validated recipe.

Iran
IRN · compound 145.0 · 5 recipes
MOCK
Foreign Intervention· 98Political Repression· 81Mass Protest· 72Sanctions & Arms Embargo· 79Currency Crisis· 64

Iran is the densest single-state compound case currently on the board, lighting up on foreign_intervention, political_repression, sanctions_and_arms_embargo, mass_protest, and currency_crisis : three L1 families stacking on the same body politic at once (B-Conflict, C-Governance, D-Economic). The structural read is that the same indicator pressures : degraded regulatory quality, narrowing civil-liberties space, and deteriorating fiscal-source diversity : are simultaneously the inputs that drive the foreign-intervention trajectory AND the protest-and-repression cycle, so the compound score is partly a measurement of indicator-overlap rather than three independent crisis paths. None of the lit recipes are graduated under the current honesty regime, so this is best read as a positioning aggregate, not a forecast of any single event firing : and certainly not of all five firing jointly.

Sudan
SDN · compound 86.2 · 5 recipes
MOCK
Civil War & Insurgency· 71Humanitarian Crisis· 78Foreign Intervention· 66Drought & Famine· 62Mass Protest· 59

Sudan stacks across five recipes : civil_war_and_insurgency, humanitarian_crisis, foreign_intervention, drought_and_famine, mass_protest : spanning four L1 families (B-Conflict, E-Social, G-Humanitarian plus a structural drought channel under H). Unlike the Iran case, the Sudan stack reflects genuinely distinct structural pressures rather than a single indicator bundle: conflict-shape indicators (state-capacity collapse, ethnic-violence rates) sit alongside meteorological and humanitarian-throughput signals that draw on a non-overlapping data substrate. This is the architecturally cleanest compound case in the current vintage, and the one most consistent with the original intuition of the compound page : three independent lenses pointing at the same country.

Argentina
ARG · compound 84.5 · 4 recipes
MOCK
Currency Crisis· 82Economic Recession· 72Mass Protest· 62Fiscal Policy Change· 68

Argentina shows the canonical economic-compound stack: currency_crisis, economic_recession, fiscal_policy_change, and mass_protest : three D-Economic recipes plus an E-Social spillover. The reading to be careful about here is that currency_crisis and economic_recession share substantial indicator overlap (inflation, GDP growth, fiscal-source diversity), so a high compound score is mathematically expected rather than informative; the mass_protest light-up is the one independent confirmation that the economic stress is propagating into the social domain rather than being absorbed at the policy layer. Until at least one of these recipes graduates and earns a confidence band, the Argentina case is a worked example of why compound scores need same-family-discount handling in v2.
